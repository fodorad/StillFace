StillFace¶

StillFace is a toolkit for the analysis of mother-baby interaction recorded with the Still Face paradigm. A session consists of several synchronized camera views, heart rate variability recordings and annotations, and is divided into the baseline, play, stillface and reunion phases. The package prepares the raw recordings and provides the building blocks for extracting behavioral features such as touch, affect and gaze between mother and baby.

Data layout¶

The original recordings are never modified. The data/ folder of a checkout holds two soft links:

data/
├── DB            -> <original dataset>/ELTE-PPK_StillFace      (read-only)
└── DB_PROCESSED  -> <processed dataset>/ELTE-PPK_StillFace     (generated)

All generated files are written below DB_PROCESSED:

Sessions/<id>/normalized/   sources converted to mp4 at 60 fps
Sessions/<id>/synced/       time-aligned camera videos
Sessions/<id>/cut/          per-phase clips
Sessions/<id>/visualize/    stacked overview videos
Sessions/<id>/thumbnail.png
Thumbnails/                 one thumbnail per session
logs/                       synced / failed session bookkeeping

The locations can be overridden with the STILLFACE_RAW_DIR and STILLFACE_PROCESSED_DIR environment variables or the --raw-dir / --processed-dir options.

Analysis stack¶

The coding of a session (touch, emotion, gaze) rests on a shared analysis of the scene: who is where, in which camera, in 3D, and what is heard. All models are pretrained; nothing is trained on this dataset. The stack was built in three probing rounds on the probing set above plus two more sessions (616106, the clearest four-camera session of a screen of all 48, and 665814, the session with the most crying in play). Quality is measured without labels (reprojection, temporal stability, agreement between independent methods, the still-face effect) and by visual review; human labels (Annie) come next. Outputs are stored under DB_PROCESSED/ELTE-PPK_StillFace/probes/.

Body meshes are drawn on 97-100% of the frames (636211 mother: 52%, nothing during the window zoom) (baby 2D error in the baby camera 5-15 px); the mother’s side-camera error matches round 2 (626021: 26 / 24 px) at a much steadier pose.

Decisions made on the fly

  • Depth at 15 Hz instead of 60 fps: 60 fps would take more than 2 h per session on the Mac mini M4.

  • VGGT could not be installed for two new sessions; their cameras start from Depth Anything 3’s own pose estimates (12 views). This works but gives weaker calibrations (616106: 20-46 px).

  • Per-frame depth anchoring is used per person only when it beats the global scale on held-out joints (it does not where the calibration is weak).

  • The mother’s hips are one static 3D point per seat segment, fitted to all cameras over the whole segment: her own camera sees the baby and the bouncer at hip level, so depth there measured the occluder (hips were 320-400 px off in the side views; now 8-43 px).

  • Every distance in cm uses one prior for the baby’s ear-to-ear width (11.0 cm). The body fit confounds size and distance (it implied 13-15 cm on the weaker calibrations).

  • Body fits that disagree with the 3D targets by more than one head width are not drawn.

Hard cases / failure modes

  • Occlusion: the mother leaning over the baby hides the baby in the door view (594801) and her own hips everywhere.

  • During the window zoom 636211 has only one valid view of the mother, so her body is missing for 29 s.

  • Depth errors along the close camera’s ray show up as lateral offsets in the side views; anchoring halves them where triangulation is good, but a heavy tail remains (636211 mother arms: 90th percentile 3.8 head widths).

  • The baby camera sits 15-40 cm from the mother’s head: close, blurred partial views of her.

  • The adult-trained head-pose model and the 3D head axes disagree for the baby (median 5-17 deg on the baby camera, up to 110 deg for the mother seen from behind).

Round 2, 2 fps (frames with touch: 3D proximity / pose hand on the baby / detected hand on the baby / fused): 605317 118 / 83 / 85 / 121 of 124; 626021 63 / 52 / 68 / 98; 819104 109 / 120 / 117 / 123; 594801 116 / 56 / 117 / 120; 636211 0 / 77 / 78 / 103; 656531 – / 17 / 66 / 66.

Which is better for touch: 2D poses, 3D triangulation, or 3D meshes and depth?

  • 2D contact in the baby camera sees most touches (the baby camera faces the baby, hands are large) but cannot tell a hand in front of the baby from a hand on the baby. Bare hands without a skeleton are only found by the hand detector.

  • Triangulated 3D is the most precise where it exists, but the mother’s hands are triangulated on only 4-10% of the frames: it confirms, it cannot cover.

  • Depth-completed 3D skeletons cover 27-38% of the mother’s hand frames, but their depth error (2-4 cm median, heavy tail) is close to the 5.5 cm touch threshold, so they over-call touch where calibration is weak.

  • Depth contact (hand surface vs the baby’s surface around it) does not separate touch from no touch: the depth difference is about the same in both (626021: 7.4 vs 8.1 cm; 616106: 6.0 vs 4.3 cm). At 504 px the hand on the baby is a few depth pixels and blurs into the baby.

  • Body meshes give the right definition (skin to skin) and are drawn on almost every frame, but their hands are as good as the skeleton they are fitted to; they are not yet used as a touch measure.

Current answer: the main measure (triangulated 3D, else 2D contact in the baby camera) stays the reference for precision. The 3D skeleton distance is the best candidate to add coverage where calibration is good (kappa 0.53 on 616106). The decision needs labelled frames: a frame-level review of the disagreements in Annie.

Failure modes: hands under blankets or behind the baby (baby camera framing, 605317, 819104); the mother’s arm reaching over the baby from the top of the baby camera; 2D contact through occlusion (hand in front); bare hands with no skeleton (hand detector only); only two views on the baby in 636211.

Compute and outputs¶

On a Mac mini M4 (seconds per camera frame): Grounding DINO persons 2.8, hands 1.1; RTMW-x 0.25; Gaze-LLE 0.36-0.65; YOLO11 faces about 0.13; Depth Anything 3 about 2 s per 4-view frame. One session (4 cameras, 4 phases) takes about 8-10 GPU hours, so a full run uses a GPU machine. Per session and phase the outputs are per-frame values and segments (CSV / JSON for Annie) plus review videos: the four cameras with 2D overlays, the 3D panel (skeletons and shaded body meshes from the door / window direction and from above, the hand-baby distance in cm), action units with names and head pose, and head-pose videos per camera with frame numbers.

Installation¶

ffmpeg has to be available on the PATH.

git clone https://github.com/fodorad/StillFace
cd StillFace
make dev        # virtual environment, editable install, pre-commit hooks

Usage¶

stillface prepare check-missing                       # report recordings flagged as missing
stillface prepare sync --visualize                    # synchronize all pending sessions
stillface prepare sync --session-id 575613            # a single session
stillface prepare cut --visualize                     # cut phases and render overviews
stillface prepare thumbnails                          # session thumbnails
stillface features heads --session-id 605317          # mother and baby head boxes, overlays

Sessions are selected from metadata_database.xlsx: automatic processing is skipped for rows with Auto = n, and synchronization for rows with a manually entered mother-baby offset.

Head detection¶

Gaze, affect and touch features are computed per person, so every camera clip first gets a head box for the mother and the baby on each analysed frame. The same pipeline runs on all four cameras and all phases: a text-prompted head detector (Grounding DINO, prompt “a human head.”) and a face detector (YOLO11s) are run, their tracks are fused into one track per head, and simple rules decide which track is the mother and which is the baby: on the close-up cameras the owner is the head whose face is detected and lies near the image centre, on the side cameras the two strongest heads are told apart by the fixed seating of each phase. Frames without a reliable head box are marked as not codable with the reason, and every row records whether the face was visible (the frame-wise usability mask).

The diagram sources are in docs/mermaid/; make diagrams renders them.
